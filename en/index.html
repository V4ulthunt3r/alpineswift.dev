macOS · Menu bar

LidShift

Close your MacBook, trigger an effect: a native menu bar app with animations rendered in Metal.

SwiftUIMetalmacOS 15Paid app

On the Mac App Store

Engineering & background
Idea
Closing a MacBook is the least remarkable moment of the day. LidShift turns it into a small performance.
Solution
The app reads the MacBook’s real hinge angle and lays an effect over the screen: frosted glass, Glass Bend, Shutter or a fading CRT. All of it controlled from the menu bar.
Engineering
SwiftUI for the menu bar, effects rendered in Metal. The hard part: reading the angle continuously and drawing a full-screen animation without the battery or the fans noticing. Plus a paid product, including review, pricing and sales through the Mac App Store.
Status
On the Mac App Store since November 2026, version 1.0, macOS 15 and later.

iPhone · iPad · Game

Pulse Orbit

One finger, shifting orbits: an arcade game with its own mechanic, boss fights and unlockable ships.

SwiftGame development60 fps

On the App Store

Engineering & background
Idea
An arcade game with a mechanic of its own: instead of steering, you switch orbit. One finger, one rule, understood in seconds.
Solution
Steer a ship through orbits, collect stars, dodge asteroids and laser traps, unlock new ships and take on bosses.
Engineering
Game mechanics, enemies and power-ups in Swift. A game forgives no stutter: every frame has to land, on older iPhones too. That discipline later pays off in every app that has to scroll smoothly.
Status
On the App Store since October 2025 and developed on to version 1.4.1 since. The longest stretch of long haul in the studio’s own portfolio.

Can you take over an existing app?

Yes. We first review the code, access requirements and your goals. Then we can assess whether you need individual fixes, help with launch or ongoing development.

How much does an app cost?

That depends on scope, platforms and what already exists. After our initial discussion, you receive an estimate. If you have a budget range, share it so we can shape the first version accordingly.

Who owns the app, code and store account?

The app is published through your own developer account. You retain ownership of the app and all revenue. We set out code handover, access and usage rights in the proposal.

What happens after launch?

I can continue to look after your app: bug fixes, operating-system updates, new features and store maintenance. We agree the scope to suit your project.
